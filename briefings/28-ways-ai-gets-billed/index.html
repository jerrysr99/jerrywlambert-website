28 Ways AI Gets Billed

Every pricing mechanism I can document across 106 models from 18 vendors, ranked from most common to most obscure, with what to watch, how to optimize and what to avoid for each one.

Executive Summary

I track 106 priced models from 18 vendors. Across them I count 28 distinct ways a vendor turns usage into a charge. Two are close to universal. Every model prices input and output tokens, and nearly every model discounts cached input. Everything else is a twist on those two meters, and the twists are where budgets break.

The common twists change the price of the same token. A cache write costs 1.25x input on Anthropic, the same as input at most vendors, and nothing at all on many paid models. Batch cuts the price in half. Speed tiers push it the other way. OpenAI Fast is 2x standard, Anthropic fast mode takes Claude Opus 5.5 from $4/$20 to $8/$40, and OpenAI Ultrafast reaches 6x. On some models a prompt-size band reprices the whole request once the prompt crosses a line: above 272,000 tokens on OpenAI, at 200,000 on xAI and Gemini 2.5 Pro, above 100,000 on Claude Haiku 5.5. Data residency adds 10 percent at Anthropic and OpenAI, and on Claude served through regional Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud endpoints.

The less common twists add new meters. Web search costs $10 per 1,000 calls at Anthropic and OpenAI. Z.ai and MiniMax charge $0.01 per call, which works out to the same price. Google charges $14 per 1,000 after 5,000 free requests a month. Code containers bill by the container-hour or by the 20-minute session. Gemini charges $0.50 per million tokens per hour to keep an explicit cache alive. DeepSeek halves its prices outside two weekday UTC windows and treats every Chinese public holiday as off-peak. Prices also move on a calendar. Gemini 4 Argon doubles on December 1, 2026, and Gemini 3.8, 3.7 and 3.6 Flash double on January 1, 2027. Claude 4.7 and later use a tokenizer that produces about 30 percent more tokens for the same text, which raises the bill without touching the price.

At the far end sit mechanisms that never show up as a token price. Claude Pro and Max ration usage in five-hour windows, and Claude Duet, which runs only on Opus 5.5, draws on those same plan limits. Claude Enterprise charges $20 per user per month plus API rates. OpenRouter takes 5 percent when you bring your own key. Enterprise contracts add take-or-pay commits, and some vendors sell prepaid points that expire.

My operating rule is simple. Every mechanism needs a meter, a dated price and a stacking rule. If a billing system cannot meter a mechanism, it should refuse the request that triggers it instead of billing it at the wrong price. Bill a 1-hour cache write on Moonshot Kimi K3.1 Pro at the 5-minute price and you collect $2.00 of the $4.00 owed on that line. Let a web search through unmetered and you lose $10 for every 1,000 calls.

How I Counted

I worked from my live Tariff as read on October 24, 2026: 106 priced models from 18 vendors (Alibaba, Amazon, Anthropic, Cohere, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, Microsoft, MiniMax, Mistral, Moonshot, OpenAI, StepFun, Tencent, TypeSafe, Xiaomi, xAI, Z.ai).

I ranked each mechanism by how many of the tracked models can produce it, most common first. For mechanisms my Tariff stores directly (token prices, cache prices, prompt-size bands, peak and off-peak bands, $0 lines, scheduled step-ups, per-request fees), that means the models that carry the price line. For mechanisms vendors publish but my Tariff does not store as a separate line (batch, speed tiers, web search, data residency, storage, runtime), it means the models whose vendor's own pricing page offers it.

Mechanisms outside per-model API pricing (media units, training, subscriptions, per-user licenses, commits and similar) come last, in their original order. The ranking measures how widely a mechanism can produce a charge, not how often the charge appears on invoices. The 106 models are the set I track, not the whole market.

Prices are US dollars per million tokens unless stated. A pair such as $4/$20 means $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. Any claim I could not confirm against a vendor's own page or my live Tariff carries the label UNVERIFIED.

The Mechanisms

01
Name · Mechanism 1 of 28

Per Input and Output Token

Description

This is the base meter. The vendor prices input (prompt) tokens and output (completion) tokens separately, per million. Output almost always costs more.

Example

Claude Opus 5.5 lists $4.00 input and $20.00 output. Across the 106 models I track, input runs from $0.03 (Z.ai GLM-OCR) to $30.00 (OpenAI gpt-5.5-pro), and output tops out at $180.00, also on gpt-5.5-pro. The output-to-input ratio clusters at a few values. 5x is the most common, then 4x, 6x and 3x.

Models That Use It

Every vendor I track.

Things to Watch

  • Output-heavy work such as agents, code generation and long reports is priced on the expensive side. At a 5x ratio, one output token costs as much as five input tokens.
  • Token counts do not compare cleanly across vendors, or even across generations of one vendor's models (see Mechanism 13).
  • Some vendors price output at $0. TypeSafe Jev bills $0.042 input and $0 output.

Ways to Optimize

  • Cap output length with a max-tokens setting and ask for structured, terse output.
  • Route by task. Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 against Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 is a 40x spread inside one vendor.

Things to Avoid

  • Comparing models on input price alone.
  • Treating a list price as the effective price. Nearly every mechanism that follows changes it.

Name · Mechanism 2 of 28

Cached Input Read Discount

Description

When a request reuses a prompt prefix the vendor already holds in cache, those tokens bill at a cache-hit price below the normal input price.

Example
Vendor and modelCache read price
Anthropic, most models0.1x input
Anthropic Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.50.05x input
Anthropic Fable 5.10.025x input ($0.25 against $10.00)
DeepSeek Flash, off-peak$0.003 cached against $0.15 uncached (98 percent off)

Across tracked models the most common ratio is 0.1x, then 0.2x and 0.25x.

Models That Use It

Every vendor except TypeSafe. A few models price a cache read at the full input price (several Alibaba models, plus OpenAI gpt-5.5-pro and o3-pro). Some paid models publish no cache price at all (TypeSafe models and some Z.ai models). Google Gemma 4 12B and 31B carry all four price lines (input, output, cache read and cache write) at $0.

Things to Watch

  • Discounts vary 30-fold, from 0.0083x (Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6-Pro) to 0.25x. Fewer than half of the discounting models charge exactly 0.1x, so a blanket "90 percent off cached input" assumption is wrong for most of them, and more so once you add the at-par, unpriced and free models.
  • On Anthropic, a cache hit also refreshes the cache timer.
  • Some models cache only implicitly. Google says Gemini 4 Argon supports "implicit caching only (explicit caching is not supported)."

Ways to Optimize

  • Put stable content first (system prompt, tool definitions, reference documents) and variable content last, so the prefix matches.
  • Keep a conversation on one model. Caches do not carry across models.

Things to Avoid

  • Reordering tools or injecting a timestamp at the top of the prompt. Either one breaks the prefix.
  • Billing a model with no published cache price as if it had one. A cache price with no first-party basis is an invented number.

Per-Call Server Tool Fees

Description

When the vendor runs a tool on its own servers (web search, grounding, file search), it charges per call or per 1,000 calls. That fee sits on top of the tokens the tool's results add to the prompt.

On Google, one customer request can trigger several billed searches.

Models That Use It

Z.ai, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI, Xiaomi, DeepSeek and MiniMax. Moonshot also prices its built-in tools, but I have not checked the rate.

Things to Watch

  • The fee is per call, not per answer. One prompt can fan out into many searches.
  • The tool's results come back as input tokens and stay in context on later turns.
  • Free allowances are shared across models and reset monthly or daily, depending on the model family.

Ways to Optimize

  • Cap tool calls per request.
  • Prefer token-only tools where the vendor offers them, such as Anthropic web fetch and Google URL context.

Things to Avoid

  • Letting a server tool through a billing layer that cannot count it. If the call count is not metered, refuse the tool instead of billing tokens only.

Separate Cache Write Charge

Description

The vendor bills the cache write as its own line. Depending on the vendor, the write costs more than input, the same as input, less than input, or nothing.

Example
TreatmentExample
PremiumAnthropic 5-minute write at 1.25x input: Opus 5.5 $5.00 against $4.00
PremiumOpenAI newer models, 30-minute write at 1.25x: gpt-6-astra $12.50 against $10.00
DiscountDeepSeek V4 Pro write $0.435 against $1.32 peak input. UNVERIFIED. DeepSeek's current page publishes no write price; my Tariff carries the figure from an earlier source.
FreeZ.ai lists cached input storage as "Limited-time Free" on all paid GLM models
FreeXiaomi lists "Cache Write: Limited-time Free"
Models That Use It

Anthropic, OpenAI and Alibaba charge a premium for the write. Some vendors price it below input, as in the DeepSeek example. Z.ai, Xiaomi and Amazon Nova charge nothing for it. Most other models price the write at par with input, and TypeSafe jev-1.13.0 and jev-1.14.0 have no cache-write line at all.

Things to Watch

  • A write premium means caching loses money if nothing reads the prefix again. Anthropic says a 5-minute write pays off after one read and a 1-hour write after two.
  • "Limited-time free" has no end date on either the Z.ai page or the Xiaomi page.

Ways to Optimize

  • Cache only the prefixes you expect to reuse within the retention window.

Things to Avoid

  • Treating a free write as permanent. Record it as a promotion and check it again at every price review.

Batch Discount

Description

You submit work asynchronously and accept a delay, typically up to 24 hours. In exchange you pay roughly half.

Example
ModelStandardBatch
Anthropic Opus 5.5$4/$20$2/$10 (50 percent off input and output)
OpenAI gpt-6-astra$10/$50$5/$25
Google Gemini 4 Argon$2/$10$1/$5
Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6-Pro$0.435/$0.87$0.2175/$0.435
Models That Use It

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Xiaomi. Mistral's pricing page has a Batch tab, but I did not extract its rates, so Mistral batch pricing is UNVERIFIED. xAI says "not every model accepts Batch API requests," and its batch rates are UNVERIFIED.

Things to Watch

  • On Anthropic, the batch discount stacks with caching.
  • Not every model or mode qualifies. Xiaomi Ultraspeed, Anthropic fast mode and Anthropic Managed Agents have no batch option.
  • OpenAI's batch table omits cached-input discounts for several older models, gpt-4.1 among them.

Ways to Optimize

  • Move evaluations, backfills, classification and nightly reports to batch.

Things to Avoid

  • Using batch for anything a user is waiting on.

Name · Mechanism 6 of 28

Free Tiers and $0 Price Lines

Description

The vendor prices a model, a unit or a usage band at zero, either permanently or for a limited time.

Example
  • Fully free models. Google Gemma 4 is free tier only and listed as "Not available" on paid. I track two sizes, 12B and 31B. Z.ai also lists GLM-4.7-Flash, GLM-4.5-Flash and GLM-4.6V-Flash as free, but I do not track those three.
  • Free units. TypeSafe Jev output is $0. Amazon Nova cache writes are $0. Z.ai and Xiaomi cache writes are free for a limited time.
  • Free allowances. Anthropic code execution includes 1,550 free hours per organization per month. Google grounding includes 5,000 requests per month. OpenAI includes 1 GB of file storage, and OpenAI moderation is free.
  • Free trial credit. Anthropic gives new users "a small amount of free credits."
Models That Use It

Z.ai, TypeSafe, Amazon, Xiaomi and Google carry $0 price lines. Gemma 4 12B and Gemma 4 31B are entirely free. Z.ai's free Flash models are on the vendor's list but not among the models I track.

Things to Watch

  • A free tier can carry a data cost. Google's free tier states "Content used to improve our products."
  • A $0 line with no first-party source looks exactly like a missing price.

Ways to Optimize

  • Use free allowances for development and evaluation traffic.

Things to Avoid

  • Recording an unknown price as $0. A $0 line should carry its source and the vendor's own word "free."

  • Pick the smallest memory class that does the job.

  • Attaching files "just in case" on Anthropic. The attachment starts the meter.

Name · Mechanism 9 of 28

Data Residency and Regional Price Lists

Description

The vendor adds a surcharge to keep processing inside a named geography, or publishes a separate price list for each region.

Example
  • Anthropic's inference_geo: "us" setting is 1.1x on every token category for Claude 4.6 and later.
  • Claude on Amazon Bedrock regional endpoints and Google Cloud multi-region endpoints carries a 10 percent premium over global endpoints.
  • OpenAI regional processing and FedRAMP endpoints carry a 10 percent uplift for models released on or after March 5, 2026.
  • Xiaomi publishes separate China (RMB) and overseas (USD) tables. MiMo-V2.6-Pro input is ¥3.00 on one and $0.435 on the other. The Tencent Hunyuan prices in my Tariff are Singapore-region prices. Alibaba Model Studio publishes an International (Singapore) list.
Models That Use It

Claude models with US-only inference, plus models priced from a region-specific list at Alibaba, Xiaomi and Tencent. Which OpenAI models carry the uplift depends on each model's release date, and that list is UNVERIFIED.

Things to Watch

  • The surcharge multiplies every other mechanism, including fast mode and long-context bands.

Ways to Optimize

  • Use residency only for data that requires it, and route the rest globally.

Things to Avoid

  • Accepting a residency parameter on a route that bills global rates.

Prompt-Size (Long Context) Bands

Description

When the prompt crosses a threshold, the whole request reprices at a higher band. The higher price applies to every token in the request, not only the tokens above the line.

Example
ModelThresholdStandardAbove threshold
OpenAI gpt-5.5Above 272,000 input tokens$5/$30$10/$45
xAI grok-4.7200,000 prompt tokens or more$2.00/$6.00$4.00/$12.00, "for all tokens in the request"
Google Gemini 2.5 ProAbove 200,000$1.25/$10$2.50/$15
Anthropic Claude Haiku 5.5Above 100,000 (cache reads count toward it)$0.10/$0.50$0.50/$2.50 (5x)
MiniMax M3Above 512,000$0.30/$1.20$0.60/$2.40
Alibaba Qwen 3.7 PlusAbove 256,000$0.40/$1.60$1.20/$4.80

The counter-example is Anthropic. Claude 4.6 and later, except Haiku 5.5, bill the full 1M-token window at standard rates.

Models That Use It

OpenAI, xAI, Google, Anthropic (Haiku 5.5 only), MiniMax and Alibaba.

Things to Watch

  • One token over the line can double the cost of the entire request.
  • Thresholds differ by vendor, and so does the boundary. OpenAI's line is ">272K" and xAI's is "≥200K".

Ways to Optimize

  • Trim or summarize context to stay under the threshold.
  • Retrieve the passages a request needs instead of stuffing whole documents into the prompt.

Things to Avoid

  • Estimating cost from a blended average. Price each request against its own band.

Cache Write Priced by Retention Time (TTL)

Description

The longer the vendor holds the cache, the more the write costs.

Example
ModelShort retention writeLong retention write
Anthropic Opus 5.55-minute: $5 (1.25x input)1-hour: $8 (2x input)
OpenAI gpt-6-astra30-minute: $12.5012-hour: $25.00 (2x the 30-minute)
Moonshot kimi-k3.1-pro5-minute: $2.001-hour: $4.00
Moonshot kimi-k3.1-pro Highspeed5-minute: $4.001-hour: $8.00
Moonshot kimi-k35-minute: $3.001-hour: $6.00

Moonshot selects the retention time with a request option, prompt_cache_options.ttl.

Models That Use It

Anthropic, OpenAI and Moonshot.

Things to Watch

  • A platform that stores only one write column bills the long-retention write at the short-retention price. On Kimi K3.1 Pro that is $2.00 billed against $4.00 owed.
  • Each vendor uses a different request field to select retention, so a check written for one vendor's field misses another's.

Ways to Optimize

  • Use the long retention only when the gaps between reuses exceed the short window. On Anthropic it needs two reads to break even.

Things to Avoid

  • Metering one retention time and passing the other through unpriced. Either price every retention column or refuse the long-retention option.

Flex (Slow, Cheap Synchronous) Tier

Description

The request stays synchronous but accepts slower or interruptible service in exchange for a batch-like discount.

Example
ModelStandardFlex
OpenAI gpt-6-astra$10/$50$5/$25
Google Gemini 4 Argon$2/$10$1/$5
Models That Use It

OpenAI and Google. Whether Gemini 3.7 Flash offers batch, Flex and Priority is UNVERIFIED. Google's page does not show it as its own section, so I leave it out.

Things to Watch

  • Flex trades availability for price. Expect slower responses or capacity errors.

  • Use Flex for non-urgent synchronous jobs that cannot be restructured as batch.

Name · Mechanism 13 of 28

Hidden Token Overhead

Description

These are tokens you pay for but did not write: tool system prompts, tool definitions, tokenizer changes and fixed blocks.

Example
  • Anthropic adds a tool-use system prompt of 286 tokens (Opus 5.5) to 675 tokens (Opus 4.7) whenever tools are present. The computer-use toolset adds about 4,500 input tokens and the browser toolset about 6,600.
  • Claude 4.7 and later use a tokenizer that "produces approximately 30% more tokens for the same text."
  • OpenAI bills web search content for gpt-4o-mini and gpt-4.1-mini as a fixed block of 8,000 input tokens per call.
Models That Use It

Every tracked Claude model carries tool overhead, and the newer Claude models also use the newer tokenizer. OpenAI gpt-4.1-mini falls under the fixed 8,000-token search block. The other model named in that OpenAI rule, gpt-4o-mini, is not one I track.

Things to Watch

  • A model with a lower list price can cost more per task if it emits 30 percent more tokens.

Ways to Optimize

  • Compare models on cost per completed task, not cost per token.
  • Declare only the tools a request needs.

Things to Avoid

  • Carrying old per-token benchmarks across a tokenizer change.

Name · Mechanism 14 of 28

Reasoning (Thinking) Tokens Billed as Output

Description

Internal reasoning tokens bill at the output price, even when the vendor hides or encrypts them.

Example
  • Google labels every Gemini output price "including thinking tokens." Gemini 4 Argon output is $10.00.
  • Alibaba Qwen 3.6-35B-A3B lists one $2.25 output price for "thinking and non-thinking."
  • xAI's Responses API always returns encrypted reasoning content for grok-4.7.
Models That Use It

Paid Gemini models, whose prices carry the "including thinking tokens" label, and Alibaba Qwen 3.6-35B-A3B. In practice it covers most models that can reason. No tracked vendor publishes a separate thinking-token price.

Things to Watch

  • Reasoning effort settings change output volume, often by multiples, at the same unit price.
  • DeepSeek defaults to thinking mode.

Ways to Optimize

  • Set the lowest reasoning effort that passes your quality bar.
  • Use non-thinking modes for simple tasks.

Things to Avoid

  • Forecasting output cost from the length of the visible answer.

  • Carrying promotional prices into budgets or contracts past the stated end date.

Per-User License and Bring-Your-Own-Key Fees

Description

The customer pays a fixed fee per user, or pays a platform fee when it supplies its own vendor API key (bring your own key, or BYOK).

Example
  • Claude Enterprise charges $20 per user per month, plus usage billed at API rates.
  • OpenRouter BYOK charges 5 percent of what the same model would cost on OpenRouter, after a pay-as-you-go allowance of $25,000 of list-price inference per month.
Models That Use It

Anthropic, OpenRouter, and resellers generally.

Things to Watch

  • With BYOK, the vendor bills the customer directly and the platform's fee sits on top.
  • A request that falls back from the customer's key to the platform's shared capacity is billed to the platform account.

Ways to Optimize

  • Match the pricing model to volume. Per-user pricing favors heavy users, and a percentage fee favors light ones.

Things to Avoid

  • Mixing resale and BYOK billing in the same billing cycle. A BYOK request that lands on the platform's own key should be blocked, not silently billed either way. This is a design principle, not a vendor price.

Resale Markup and Reseller Chains

Description

A reseller prices on top of vendor cost, and resellers can sit under other resellers.

Example

A worked illustration, not a vendor price.

LevelCost to this levelSells atMargin
Vendor list$8.00
Platform, 25 percent markup$8.00$10.00$2.00

Each level earns $2.00. Each level's markup applies to the price that level pays, never to vendor list.

Models That Use It

Aggregators and resellers.

Things to Watch

  • A model a reseller has not priced should be refused at that level, not fall through to the parent's price.

Ways to Optimize

  • Keep a dated price trail from vendor list to end customer for every call.

Things to Avoid

  • Applying a markup to vendor list when the reseller's actual cost is the parent's price.

How the Mechanisms Stack

Vendors publish how their multipliers combine. On Anthropic, cache multipliers stack on fast mode, data residency stacks on fast mode, batch and caching combine, and fast mode is not available with batch.

Anthropic's own worked example: a one-hour Managed Agents session on Opus 5.5 with 50,000 input tokens and 15,000 output tokens costs $0.58 ($0.20 input, $0.30 output, $0.08 runtime). If 40,000 of those input tokens are cache reads, the session costs $0.428.

The order I use for a billing system: select the prompt-size band, apply multipliers (speed, residency), apply resale rules, then volume tiers and discounts, then credits, then draw down any commit.
