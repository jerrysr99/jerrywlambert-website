Executive Summary

AI pricing keeps getting more complicated. I now track 1,934 models and count 35 distinct ways a vendor turns usage into a charge. Charges range from the per-token meter to HDR surcharges, download fees, capacity units, router models with no price of their own, and license terms that flip from free to paid at a revenue line.

Vendors are also cutting their old models loose. 12.8% of my full tracked population is now retired. Another 22.2% are open-weights models you host yourself, with no vendor price at all, and 10.8% have no public price. A retired model takes its price with it.

The per-token meter still dominates. 82.7% of priced models bill input and output tokens, and the priced vendors that skip it are all media vendors. Half of priced models (50.4%) discount cached input, and 63.6% of priced vendors do. Reasoning billed at the output price reaches 42.5% of priced models. Media units rank third by vendor share. 54.5% of priced vendors bill by the image, the second, the minute or the character, and 35.0% of priced models carry one of those units.

The twists are where budgets break. Server tool fees cover 34.3% of priced models. Web search costs $10 per 1,000 calls at Anthropic and OpenAI, and Google charges $14 per 1,000 after 5,000 free requests a month. Batch discounts cover 33.1%. Regional price lists cover 29.5%, and prompt-size bands that reprice a whole request cover 23.2%. Speed tiers run the other way. OpenAI Fast is 2x standard and OpenAI Ultrafast reaches 6x.

The new mechanisms add meters that a token-only bill misses. Luma prices HDR video at 2x SDR and HDR plus EXR at 3x. xAI charges $0.20 per GiB to download stored files. Luma sells Uni-1.1 throughput in capacity units with a minimum of four. Liquid AI lets businesses under $10M in annual revenue run its open-weights models in production at no cost. xAI sells Grok 4.7 Fast only through Cursor and Grok Build. Alibaba will not combine batch and cache discounts, and xAI applies the cache discount before the priority multiplier.

My operating rule is simple. Every mechanism needs a meter, a dated price and a stacking rule. If a billing system cannot meter a mechanism, it should refuse the request that triggers it instead of billing it at the wrong price. Bill a 1-hour cache write on Moonshot Kimi K3.1 Pro at the 5-minute price and you collect $2.00 of the $4.00 owed on that line. Let a web search through unmetered and you lose $10 for every 1,000 calls.

How I Counted

I track 1,934 models. For every model with a published price, I recorded each mechanism that the vendor's public announcements show for that model.

Every percentage is a share of the full tracked population's priced models, or of the vendors behind them. I rank by the model share, most common first, and break ties on the vendor share. Media-only vendors cannot show token mechanisms such as cache discounts or reasoning tokens, which holds those shares down.

All 35 are numbered in one list. Numbers 1 to 27 are ranked by how often they show up on a model's own price row. Numbers 28 to 35 are plan and contract-level charges. They live on plan pages, in contracts or behind access tiers, so a share of priced models does not describe them, and the table marks them "Plan or contract level" instead.

Prices are US dollars per million tokens unless stated. A pair such as $4/$20 means $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens.

The Mechanisms

01
Name · Mechanism 1 of 35

Per Input and Output Token

Description

This is the base meter. The vendor prices input (prompt) tokens and output (completion) tokens separately, per million. Output almost always costs more.

Example

Claude Opus 5.5 lists $4.00 input and $20.00 output. OpenAI gpt-5.5-pro lists $30.00 input and $180.00 output. Some vendors flip the pattern. TypeSafe Jev and Liquid d1 price output at $0.00 and bill input only.

Who Uses It

Seen on 82.7% of priced models and 81.8% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include AI21, Alibaba, Amazon, Anthropic, Baidu, ByteDance, Cohere, Cursor, DeepSeek, Google, IBM, Inception, Meta, Microsoft, MiniMax, Mistral, Moonshot, OpenAI, Perplexity, StepFun, Tencent, Thinking Machines, TypeSafe, Upstage, xAI, Xiaomi and Z.ai. The priced vendors without a token price are all media vendors.

Things to Watch

  • Output-heavy work such as code generation and long reports is priced on the expensive side. At a 5x ratio, one output token costs as much as five input tokens.
  • Token counts do not compare cleanly across vendors, or even across generations of one vendor's models (see Hidden Token Overhead).
  • An output price of $0 moves the whole bill onto the prompt. TypeSafe Jev bills $0.042 input and $0 output.

Ways to Optimize

  • Cap output length with a max-tokens setting and ask for structured, terse output.
  • Route by task. Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 against Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 is a 40x spread inside one vendor.

Things to Avoid

  • Comparing models on input price alone.
  • Treating a list price as the effective price. Nearly every mechanism that follows changes it.

Name · Mechanism 2 of 35

Cached Input Read Discount

Description

When a request reuses a prompt prefix the vendor already holds in cache, those tokens bill at a cache-hit price below the normal input price.

Example
Vendor and modelCache read price
Anthropic, most models0.1x input
Anthropic Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.50.05x input
Anthropic Fable 5.10.025x input ($0.25 against $10.00)
DeepSeek Flash, off-peak$0.003 cached against $0.15 uncached (98 percent off)
Who Uses It

Seen on 50.4% of priced models and 63.6% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include Alibaba, Amazon, Anthropic, ByteDance, Cursor, DeepSeek, Google, Inception, Meta, MiniMax, Mistral, Moonshot, OpenAI, Perplexity, StepFun, Tencent, Thinking Machines, Upstage, xAI, Xiaomi and Z.ai.

Things to Watch

  • Discounts vary 30-fold, from 0.0083x (Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6-Pro) to 0.25x. A blanket "90 percent off cached input" assumption is wrong for many models, and some models price a cache read at the full input price.
  • On Anthropic, a cache hit also refreshes the cache timer.
  • Some models cache only implicitly. Google says Gemini 4 Argon supports "implicit caching only (explicit caching is not supported)."

Ways to Optimize

  • Put stable content first (system prompt, tool definitions, reference documents) and variable content last, so the prefix matches.
  • Keep a conversation on one model. Caches do not carry across models.

Things to Avoid

  • Reordering tools or injecting a timestamp at the top of the prompt. Either one breaks the prefix.
  • Billing a model with no published cache price as if it had one. A cache price with no first-party basis is an invented number.
03
Name · Mechanism 3 of 35

Reasoning (Thinking) Tokens Billed as Output

Description

Internal reasoning tokens bill at the output price, even when the vendor hides or encrypts them.

Example
  • Google labels every Gemini output price "including thinking tokens." Gemini 4 Argon output is $10.00.
  • Alibaba Qwen 3.6-35B-A3B lists one $2.25 output price for "thinking and non-thinking."
  • xAI's Responses API always returns encrypted reasoning content for grok-4.7.
Who Uses It

Seen on 42.5% of priced models and 36.4% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include Alibaba, Anthropic, Baidu, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, MiniMax, Moonshot, OpenAI, Thinking Machines, xAI and Z.ai.

Things to Watch

  • Reasoning effort settings change output volume, often by multiples, at the same unit price.
  • DeepSeek defaults to thinking mode.

Ways to Optimize

  • Set the lowest reasoning effort that passes your quality bar.
  • Use non-thinking modes for simple tasks.

Things to Avoid

  • Forecasting output cost from the length of the visible answer.

  • The fee is per call, not per answer. One prompt can fan out into many searches.
  • The tool's results come back as input tokens and stay in context on later turns.
  • Free allowances are shared across models and reset monthly or daily, depending on the model family.

Ways to Optimize

  • Cap tool calls per request.
  • Prefer token-only tools where the vendor offers them, such as Anthropic web fetch and Google URL context.

Things to Avoid

  • Letting a server tool through a billing layer that cannot count it. If the call count is not metered, refuse the tool instead of billing tokens only.

Batch Discount

Description

You submit work asynchronously and accept a delay, typically up to 24 hours. In exchange you pay roughly half.

Example
ModelStandardBatch
Anthropic Opus 5.5$4/$20$2/$10 (50 percent off input and output)
OpenAI gpt-6-astra$10/$50$5/$25
Google Gemini 4 Argon$2/$10$1/$5
Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6-Pro$0.435/$0.87$0.2175/$0.435
Who Uses It

Seen on 33.1% of priced models and 27.3% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include Alibaba, Amazon, Anthropic, ByteDance, Google, Moonshot, OpenAI, xAI and Xiaomi.

Things to Watch

  • On Anthropic, the batch discount stacks with caching.
  • Not every model or mode qualifies. Xiaomi Ultraspeed, Anthropic fast mode and Anthropic hosted sessions have no batch option.
  • OpenAI's batch table omits cached-input discounts for several older models, gpt-4.1 among them.

Ways to Optimize

  • Move evaluations, backfills, classification and nightly reports to batch.

Things to Avoid

  • Using batch for anything a user is waiting on.

Name · Mechanism 7 of 35

Data Residency and Regional Price Lists

Description

The vendor adds a surcharge to keep inference inside a named geography, or publishes a separate price list for each region.

Example
  • Anthropic's inference_geo: "us" setting is 1.1x on every token category for Claude 4.6 and later.
  • Claude on Amazon Bedrock regional endpoints and Google Cloud multi-region endpoints carries a 10 percent premium over global endpoints.
  • OpenAI regional and FedRAMP endpoints carry an uplift for models released on or after March 5, 2026.
  • Xiaomi publishes separate China (RMB) and overseas (USD) tables. MiMo-V2.6-Pro input is ¥3.00 on one and $0.435 on the other. The Tencent Hunyuan prices in my Tariff are Singapore-region prices. Alibaba Model Studio publishes an International (Singapore) list.
  • Tencent stopped new purchases on its old Hunyuan platform and moved buyers to TokenHub, which has its own price list.
Who Uses It

Seen on 29.5% of priced models and 27.3% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include Alibaba, Amazon, Anthropic, Cursor, IBM, OpenAI, Tencent, xAI and Xiaomi.

Things to Watch

  • The surcharge multiplies every other mechanism, including fast mode and long-context bands.

Ways to Optimize

  • Use residency only for data that requires it, and route the rest globally.

Things to Avoid

  • Accepting a residency parameter on a route that bills global rates.

Prompt-Size (Long Context) Bands

Description

When the prompt crosses a threshold, the whole request reprices at a higher band. The higher price applies to every token in the request, not only the tokens above the line.

Example
ModelThresholdStandardAbove threshold
OpenAI gpt-5.5Above 272,000 input tokens$5/$30$10/$45
xAI grok-4.7200,000 prompt tokens or more$2.00/$6.00$4.00/$12.00, "for all tokens in the request"
Google Gemini 2.5 ProAbove 200,000$1.25/$10$2.50/$15
Anthropic Claude Haiku 5.5Above 100,000 (cache reads count toward it)$0.10/$0.50$0.50/$2.50 (5x)

Who Uses It

Seen on 23.2% of priced models and 27.3% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include Alibaba, Anthropic, Baidu, Black Forest Labs, ByteDance, Google, MiniMax, OpenAI and xAI.

Things to Watch

  • One token over the line can double the cost of the entire request.
  • Thresholds differ by vendor, and so does the boundary. OpenAI's line is ">272K" and xAI's is "≥200K".

Ways to Optimize

  • Trim or summarize context to stay under the threshold.
  • Retrieve the passages a request needs instead of stuffing whole documents into the prompt.

Things to Avoid

  • Estimating cost from a blended average. Price each request against its own band.

Name · Mechanism 10 of 35

Free Tiers and $0 Price Lines

Description

The vendor prices a model, a unit or a usage band at zero, either permanently or for a limited time.

Example
  • Fully free models. Google Gemma 4 12B and 31B are free tier only and listed as "Not available" on paid. Z.ai lists GLM-4.7-Flash, GLM-4.5-Flash and GLM-4.6V-Flash as free. NVIDIA runs free, rate-limited endpoints with no per-token billing.
  • Free units. TypeSafe Jev output is $0. Amazon Nova cache writes are $0. Z.ai and Xiaomi cache writes are free for a limited time.
  • Free allowances. Anthropic code execution includes 1,550 free hours per organization per month. Google grounding includes 5,000 requests per month. OpenAI includes 1 GB of file storage, and OpenAI moderation is free.
  • Free trial credit. Anthropic gives new users "a small amount of free credits."
Who Uses It

Seen on 22.4% of priced models and 18.2% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include AI21, Alibaba, Google, MiniMax, Stability AI and Z.ai. NVIDIA's build endpoints and the free variants on one large model aggregator also price at $0.

Things to Watch

  • A free tier can carry a data cost. Google's free tier states "Content used to improve our products."
  • A $0 line with no first-party source looks exactly like a missing price.

Ways to Optimize

  • Use free allowances for development and evaluation traffic.

Things to Avoid

  • Recording an unknown price as $0. A $0 line should carry its source and the vendor's own word "free."

Description

The vendor bills the cache write as its own line. Depending on the vendor, the write costs more than input, the same as input, less than input, or nothing.

Example

PremiumAnthropic 5-minute write at 1.25x input: Opus 5.5 $5.00 against $4.00

Seen on 13.8% of priced models and 15.2% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include Alibaba, Anthropic, Moonshot, OpenAI and StepFun.

Name · Mechanism 14 of 35

Billing-Event Rules

Description

These rules decide which events are billable, separate from the price itself.

Example
  • DeepSeek bills Search and Fetch per successful request.
  • Anthropic does not bill a web search that errors.
  • MiniMax does not deduct points for failed or security-blocked videos, bills the original inputs again when a video is regenerated, and charges $1.50 per cloned voice and $3 per designed voice at first use.
  • Meta bills SAM 3.1 video at a minimum frame count.
  • xAI charges $0.05 per request for a policy violation, even when it blocks the request.
  • Kimi and Perplexity bill only non-empty results. Vidu, BytePlus, Tencent and Black Forest Labs do not charge for failed or moderated jobs.
  • Runway sets minimum charges of 56 credits on aleph2 and 64 on seedance2_mini. Vidu Q2 charges 55 credits for the first second, then 15 per second.
Who Uses It

Seen on 10.2% of priced models and 18.2% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include ByteDance, Meta, MiniMax, Tencent, Vidu and xAI.

Things to Watch

  • The same price can produce different bills depending on success rules and minimums.

Ways to Optimize

  • Count only billable events in your own meter, so invoices reconcile.

Things to Avoid

  • Billing your customers for failed calls the vendor did not charge you for.

Cache Write Priced by Retention Time (TTL)

Description

The longer the vendor holds the cache, the more the write costs.

Example
ModelShort retention writeLong retention write
Anthropic Opus 5.55-minute: $5 (1.25x input)1-hour: $8 (2x input)
OpenAI gpt-6-astra30-minute: $12.5012-hour: $25.00 (2x the 30-minute)
Moonshot kimi-k3.1-pro5-minute: $2.001-hour: $4.00
Moonshot kimi-k3.1-pro Highspeed5-minute: $4.001-hour: $8.00
Moonshot kimi-k35-minute: $3.001-hour: $6.00

Moonshot selects the retention time with a request option, prompt_cache_options.ttl.

Who Uses It

Seen on 9.1% of priced models and 9.1% of priced vendors in my full tracked population. Vendors include Anthropic, Moonshot and OpenAI.

Things to Watch

  • A platform that stores only one write column bills the long-retention write at the short-retention price. On Kimi K3.1 Pro that is $2.00 billed against $4.00 owed.
  • Each vendor uses a different request field to select retention, so a check written for one vendor's field misses another's.

Ways to Optimize

  • Use the long retention only when the gaps between reuses exceed the short window. On Anthropic it needs two reads to break even.

Things to Avoid

  • Metering one retention time and passing the other through unpriced. Either price every retention column or refuse the long-retention option.

Where It Shows Up

Plan page and contract: per-seat plans and platform fees on usage, including BYOK.

Description

The customer pays a fixed fee per user, a platform fee per token on top of list price, or a fee when it supplies its own vendor API key (bring your own key, or BYOK).

  • Claude Enterprise costs US$20 per seat per month, billed annually, with a 20-seat minimum, plus usage at API rates.

Open-Weights Licenses Free Below a Revenue Threshold

Description

The vendor lets you run its open-weights models for free until your company's revenue passes a threshold, then requires a paid license.

Example

  • Building a product on free weights without reading the revenue terms.

How the Mechanisms Stack

Vendors publish how their multipliers combine. On Anthropic, cache multipliers stack on fast mode, data residency stacks on fast mode, batch and caching combine, and fast mode is not available with batch.

Anthropic's own worked example: a one-hour hosted session on Opus 5.5 with 50,000 input tokens and 15,000 output tokens costs $0.58 ($0.20 input, $0.30 output, $0.08 runtime). If 40,000 of those input tokens are cache reads, the session costs $0.428.

The order I use for a billing system: select the prompt-size band, apply multipliers (speed, residency), apply resale rules, then volume tiers and discounts, then credits, then draw down any commit.
