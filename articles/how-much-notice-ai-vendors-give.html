How Much Notice AI Vendors Give on Price and Model Changes

October 24, 2026

Lead Times on Rate Increases, Rate Decreases, New Pricing Elements and Retirements, June 2025 to October 2026

How many days first-party AI model vendors give between announcing a change and the day it takes effect, measured from 44 verified events across 11 vendors' own pages.

Retirement Notice Is Shrinking

Vendors are giving less notice before they retire a model. Across the 37 dated retirement announcements in my data, the 14 made in 2025 got a median of 150.5 days. The 23 made in 2026 so far got a median of 92 days.

Vendor by Vendor

  • Anthropic. claude-3-opus, announced Jun 30, 2025, got 189 days. claude-3-7-sonnet, announced Oct 28, 2025, got 114. Every Claude retirement announced after that got 60 to 62 days (five events), right at Anthropic's stated 60-day floor.
  • OpenAI. In 2025 OpenAI gave 365 days on the Assistants API and 367 days on legacy GPT snapshots. No 2026 announcement comes close. Its 2026 notices cluster near 90 days (92 to 97) or near 180 days (180 to 184). The exception is gpt-5.4-cyber at 20 days.
  • Mistral. mistral-ocr-4-0 got 1 day, deprecated Sep 29 and retired Sep 30, 2026.
  • DeepSeek. deepseek-v4-flash got zero notice. DeepSeek retired it on Sep 10, 2026, the day it announced the change, and routed the names to V4.1-Flash.

How I Read It

I read this as a deliberate move by the vendors. Shorter notice gives customers less time on older models, which tend to cost less than the models vendors want them on. Intent is not observable in this data. What the data shows is the shorter notice. The pricing of the retired models is not part of this study either, so the cheaper-model point is my interpretation.

Caveats. Only 37 dated events. OpenAI accounts for 22 of the 37. The 2026 figures cover about 10 months, and the latest announcement in the data is Oct 14, 2026.

What This Means for Anyone Buying AI

AI vendors change the terms of the bill constantly, and they are giving buyers less time to react. That leaves buyers with two needs.

1. A Charge Record Nobody Can Rewrite

The scale of the churn is documented. The companion report, The 28 Ways AI Models Charge, counts 28 distinct billing mechanisms across 106 models from 18 vendors. This report adds 44 verified dated events between June 2025 and October 2026: 4 rate increases, 2 rate decreases, 2 new pricing elements and 36 retirements. Some took effect the day they were announced.

When the price list moves that often, a monthly invoice cannot be trusted on its face. A buyer needs a record of every charge that cannot be altered after the fact and can be checked against the dated Tariff in force when the call ran. Each line should show the model, the units, the price applied and the Tariff date it came from. That record needs an attestation, a signed statement that it is complete and unchanged, so a finance team or an auditor can confirm it without taking anyone's word for it.

2. The Freedom to Move Work Between Vendors

The second need follows from the trend, though the data does not prove the cost effect. The logic runs like this. If vendors retire older models faster, which the data shows, and buyers must move to the replacement, and the replacement costs more per unit of work, and the buyer does not or cannot switch to a cheaper option at another vendor, then the buyer's cost per unit of work rises over time.

The third condition is not established here. This report does not compare the prices of retired models with the prices of their replacements. Price per token is also only part of the cost. Anthropic says Claude 4.7 and later use a tokenizer that "produces approximately 30% more tokens for the same text," so the same work can cost more even at an unchanged per-token price.

The fourth condition is the one a buyer controls. A buyer who can move work between vendors without rewiring an application can chase the best price per unit of capability. A buyer wired to one vendor takes whatever that vendor's next retirement notice brings, on that vendor's schedule.

My View

It is a mistake for a consumer to lock themselves into one vendor.

Executive Summary

I wanted to know how much warning AI model vendors give before a change hits my bill. I counted the days between the date a vendor printed an announcement and the date the change took effect. I found 44 verified events from 43 distinct announcements between June 2025 and October 2026. One DeepSeek repricing on August 13, 2026 counts twice, once as a rate increase and once as a new pricing element.

The answer depends on the kind of change. Model retirements get the most notice, with a median of 95.5 days across 36 events. Rate increases get weeks to months, with a median of 31.5 days across 4 events. New pricing elements get days to two weeks, with a median of 8.5 days across 2 events. Rate decreases took effect the day they were announced in both verified cases.

OpenAI is the most predictable vendor. Its median retirement notice is 181.5 days, and it publishes a 6-month policy for GA models. DeepSeek and Mistral run the shortest clocks. DeepSeek gave 3 days on a repricing that added peak-hour rates and 0 days on a retirement. Mistral gave 1 day on an OCR model retirement.

The first three categories have 2 to 4 events each. Read those numbers as indicative, not as a rule. Retirements have 36 events and give a firmer picture.

What This Means If You Manage AI Spend

  1. Increases get more notice than decreases. Every verified decrease took effect the day it was announced (2 of 2). The shortest verified increase was 3 days (DeepSeek). Both Google increases were printed at launch as intro prices with end dates 49 and 121 days out.
  2. Intro prices drive most increases. Three of the four verified increases are scheduled ends of launch pricing (Google twice, Mistral once). Put every intro end date on your calendar the day a model launches.
  3. New billable mechanisms come fast. DeepSeek added time-of-day pricing with 3 days of notice and a per-request search fee with 14 days. A weekly price check can miss a 3-day window.
  4. Retirement notice tracks the vendor's published policy. OpenAI's observed median is 181.5 days with a minimum of 20 days (gpt-5.4-cyber). Anthropic's observed minimum is 60 days and its median is 62. DeepSeek and Mistral, with no published minimum, ran from 0 to 92 days.
  5. Zero-notice events happen. Anthropic cut a cache-read price on Oct 7, 2026. DeepSeek cut V4.1-Flash prices and retired V4-Flash on Sep 10, 2026. Google delisted gemini-3.5-flash and gemini-3.7-flash prices with no notice and no shutdown date (excluded from the statistics).
  6. Some retirements have no date. Moonshot says its K2 series goes "by the end of December 2026". Anthropic lists claude-mythos-preview as "to be announced". You cannot schedule around these. You have to watch them.

Methodology

Event universe. First-party changelogs, pricing pages and deprecation pages for OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek, Mistral, Moonshot, xAI, Perplexity, Z.ai, Cohere and Microsoft, plus my own twice-daily Tariff records from Aug 27 to Oct 24, 2026.

Window. June 2025 to October 2026, where vendor pages carry dates.

Dates. The announcement date is the date the vendor printed on the changelog, release note, deprecation entry or pricing page stamp. The effective date is the vendor's stated effective, shutdown or retirement date. Days of notice equal the effective date minus the announcement date, in calendar days, using the date part only (UTC).

Counting. One row per vendor announcement batch. A batch that retires several models on one date counts once.

Exclusions. I left out events with no effective date, events whose announcement date the vendor did not print, and events whose announcement date comes only from my first sighting (marked PROXY ANNOUNCE, an upper bound). Corrections to my own Tariff are not vendor events and are excluded. Only unflagged rows and ZERO NOTICE rows enter the statistics.

Fetch dates. I fetched the OpenAI deprecations, Anthropic model deprecations, Google Gemini deprecations, DeepSeek change log, Mistral changelog and Kimi platform changelog on Oct 24, 2026. Other facts come from dated captures of the vendor page at the time.
