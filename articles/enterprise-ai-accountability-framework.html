The Enterprise AI Accountability Framework

A telecom-derived lexicon for the AI transaction economy · by Jerry W. Lambert, Sr.

Artificial intelligence is becoming a transaction economy. Every prompt, inference, retrieval, routing decision, and agent invocation is a metered economic event — and as those events multiply, enterprises will need to independently record, attribute, govern, and reconcile them, exactly as the telecommunications industry learned to do across forty years of building the largest machine-generated transaction-accountability infrastructure ever assembled.

This paper argues the AI industry should not reinvent that infrastructure — it should inherit it. It coins four primitives, each minted directly from a proven telecom ancestor, and shows that they arrive not as a co-equal stack but in a forced order, each made necessary only when scale renders the previous one insufficient.

The lexicon

  • Token Detail Record (TDR) — the atomic, per-event record of a single AI consumption charge, kept independently by both buyer and provider and reconciled against each other. Heir to the Call Detail Record.
  • Proxy of Record (POR) — the enterprise-controlled gateway that observes, governs, and records AI activity, and mints the TDR. Heir to the switch of record and the mediation layer.
  • Model Routing Engine (MRE) — the policy layer that selects the right model for each request across cost, latency, capability, geography, security, and compliance. Heir to least-cost routing.
  • Model Decision Record (MDR) — the record explaining why a given model was chosen. Heir to routing-policy provenance — telecom's last and hardest-won discipline.

The maturity sequence

The framework's spine is the order in which these arrive: record consumption → centralize control → govern selection → justify selection. An enterprise's position in that sequence tells it not only what to build, but when. Nearly every organization today is at stage one — and the discipline is in resisting the later stages until scale actually demands them.

The paper closes on standardization: telecom never fully standardized the Call Detail Record at its source, and AI is heading for the same fragmentation. Its advantage is foresight — agree the base fields of the TDR early, and a versioned standard, plausibly governed by a body like the ITU, can be administered before the formats harden.
