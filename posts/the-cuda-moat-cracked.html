The CUDA Moat Cracked

April 28, 2026 · 8:00 AM CT

Eight days. On April 15, Jensen Huang told the Dwarkesh Podcast that DeepSeek running on Huawei chips would be "a horrible outcome for our nation." On April 24, DeepSeek shipped V4 on Huawei Ascend processors. Trained, optimized, and deployed — entirely outside NVIDIA's ecosystem.

This is not a model story. This is an infrastructure story.

NVIDIA's dominance was never just the hardware. It was CUDA — twenty years of software, libraries, and developer tooling that made switching costs astronomical. Every major AI lab in the world trained on NVIDIA GPUs because the software ecosystem had no viable alternative. That was the moat.

DeepSeek rewrote its entire training pipeline from CUDA to Huawei's CANN framework. The result: a 1.6-trillion-parameter model that matches Claude and GPT-5.5 on coding benchmarks, runs at one-sixth the price, and never touched an NVIDIA chip.

The U.S. State Department responded with a global diplomatic cable warning allies about Chinese AI intellectual property theft. China rejected the claims. But the diplomatic posture tells you what Washington already understands: this is no longer a product competition. It is a technology separation.

China now has a fully sovereign AI stack. The model is DeepSeek. The chips are Huawei Ascend. The software framework is CANN. No American dependency at any layer. I am not saying what is good or bad on that. Just calling balls and strikes.

AI vendor selection is now a geopolitical decision. Act accordingly.

It is a race. Time to catch up.
