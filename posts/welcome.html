Welcome — Why I'm Writing Publicly

April 8, 2026 · 10:30 AM CT

I'm starting this space to publish field notes from inside the AI transformation. After 30+ years running technology operations — telecom, hyperscale infrastructure, SaaS, data-intensive industries — I've never seen a shift move this fast or this deep. The honest version of what's happening doesn't fit in a LinkedIn post, and it doesn't show up in the vendor decks. So I'm writing it down.

What you'll find here

Three things, mostly:

  • Operating notes from real AI workloads. What broke, what worked, what the bill looked like, and what I'd do differently next time.
  • Executive perspective on the rate of change. Models change every day. Tools change every day. Abstractions shift. The old model of layered technology management with distance from the line is dying. I'll write about what I think replaces it.
  • The economics. The newest model is always the most expensive; the previous one drops in price but keeps most of the capability. Going forward the analysis is bang per token per dollar. Any organization that doesn't yet understand this is already behind.

The premise

AI-powered change and competitive pressure are going to demand rapid reductions in technical staffing across the entire lifecycle — planning, development, QA, security, documentation, deployment. Only leaders who are hands-on enough to truly know what is possible today will have the clarity to make the right calls. If you are not immersed in AI tool changes daily, you cannot keep up. That's the premise of everything I'm going to write here.

The new paradigm is agent teams. Each team has a sphere of responsibility. Each agent is specialized via background and persona. Those specialties need a model precisely matched to their needs — a public model with per-token cost, a local model for specialized tasks, or a mix tuned to each outcome. None of this is hypothetical. I'm running it. I'll share what I learn.

Cadence

Short and frequent over long and rare. If I learn something useful on a Tuesday, you'll probably see it by Wednesday. No editorial calendar, no thought-leadership theater — just notes from the work.

Thanks for reading. If something here lands or misses, tell me.
