What Is Under Your Agentic Hood?

October 20, 2026

My premise is simple. A cloud agent should come with a cloud computer. Its own CPU, memory, and disk, there when the agent needs to keep working. That machine is the engine under the hood.

Some do. Many do not.

The chart lists twenty agentic players and what each gives you. A few provide a persistent machine. Others spin up a VM or container per session or task instead of a machine that stays. Some run only a remote browser or leave the hosting to you, so the computer column reads None. Many vendors do not publish the spec at all, so I wrote Not published rather than guess.

The figures are a mix of official vendor docs and user measurements.

Here is the part the spec sheet does not show.

These are sizes, not speed. A bigger box is not automatically a faster one, and per-core performance differs by vendor. Among the persistent machines with figures, Grok Bot has the most memory and disk, while OpenAI dots has one more core and, per Geekbench runs, likely faster ones. My Grok Bot numbers are one measurement on one account, and they could change.

Agent buyers should ask: is there a computer, how many cores, how much memory, how much disk, and does it stay between tasks. If we are ultimately buying compute, then all compute matters.

If your agentic platform is not providing you a server as part of the service, why not?
