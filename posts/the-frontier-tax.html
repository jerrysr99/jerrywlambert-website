The Frontier Tax

June 13, 2026 · 8:00 AM CT

Everyone repeats the same line: AI keeps getting cheaper. Last week the best model on the market got twice as expensive.

Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5 at $50 per million output tokens — double the price of the model it replaced. At the very top of the market, capability went up and so did the bill.

Meanwhile the floor is falling out. DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral and Llama have pushed open-weight models good enough for most enterprise work to nearly free. Two curves, moving in opposite directions.

This is the part executives keep getting wrong. They treat "AI cost" as one number trending down, and they budget like the whole stack is on a discount. It isn't. The commodity middle is collapsing toward zero. The frontier — the small slice of work that actually needs the best model — is getting more expensive, not less.

The mistake is running everything on one curve. Either you overpay by sending routine work to a frontier model, or you underperform by forcing your hardest problems onto a commodity one. The skill in 2026 is knowing which work belongs on which curve.

Map your workload. Most of it runs fine on cheap, open, self-hosted models. A few high-stakes tasks justify the frontier premium. Price them separately, architect them separately, and stop pretending it is one line on a chart.

It is a race. Time to catch up.
