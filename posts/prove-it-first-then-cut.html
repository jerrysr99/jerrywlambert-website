Prove It First, Then Cut

May 15, 2026 · 8:00 AM CT

128,000 tech workers have lost their jobs this year. Nearly half were cut because of AI.

Here is the number nobody puts on the slide: 55% of the companies that did it already regret it.

Both numbers are true. That is the problem.

The logic sounds clean in a board meeting. AI makes engineers faster — Coinbase's CEO says his team now ships in days what used to take weeks. So you cut the headcount and book the savings.

But look at the order of operations. Most of these companies cut first and deployed second. They are betting that an AI capability they have not yet proven in production will cover the work they just eliminated. That is not an efficiency strategy. That is a leap of faith with a severance line item.

I have run large cost reductions. The ones that held followed a sequence: deploy the new capability, measure the real output, then restructure around what you actually proved. The ones that blew up skipped to the restructure and hoped the rest would follow.

AI will eliminate roles. That part is real and it is happening. But "AI did it" is becoming the cover story for cuts that were coming anyway — and for bets that have not paid off yet.

If you are cutting headcount this quarter, ask one question: have we proven the replacement, or are we hoping for it?

Prove it first. Then cut. Not the other way around.

It is a race. Time to catch up.
