Meta Got the Order Wrong

June 15, 2026 · 8:00 AM CT

A month ago I wrote that companies were cutting headcount before they proved the AI could do the work. This week the most aggressive one admitted it got the order wrong.

In an internal memo, Mark Zuckerberg said Meta "made mistakes" in its AI restructuring. The numbers behind that sentence: roughly 8,000 people cut, another 7,000 reassigned to AI projects — close to a fifth of the company reshuffled on a bet. The new flat AI unit, stretched toward 50 engineers per manager, got nicknamed "the gulag" inside the building. Now he is pledging no more company-wide layoffs this year and quietly restoring the perks he stripped.

This is not a Meta story. It is a sequencing story. Meta has more capital, more compute, and more AI talent than almost anyone on earth — and it still got burned by restructuring on faith instead of proof. If they cannot shortcut the sequence, the company copying their layoff headlines on a mid-market budget certainly cannot.

Here is the order that works, and it has not changed: deploy the capability, measure the real output, then restructure around what you actually proved. Reverse those steps and you get exactly what Meta just walked back — mass reassignment, a morale crater, and a public correction.

The lesson is louder than the original move. When the best-resourced player in the game admits it cut first and proved second, treat that as your case study.

Prove it first. Still true. Now with a receipt.

It is a race. Time to catch up.
