The $638 Billion IOU

June 30, 2026 · 8:00 AM CT

Oracle just booked the largest order backlog in enterprise history — $638 billion, up 363% in a year. Almost everyone read it as a demand story. It's a financing story.

A backlog is not revenue. It's a promise you still have to pay to keep.

To deliver that $638 billion, Oracle has to build the data centers first. This year it spent $55.7 billion on capital expenditure and posted negative $23.7 billion in free cash flow. The order book is enormous. The cash to fulfill it has to be borrowed and built ahead of the money coming in.

I'm not knocking the company. I've sat in the chair where you sign multi-year infrastructure commitments. The pattern is what matters: across the industry, AI demand is being booked years before it's delivered, and the buildout is financed on debt against contracts that pay out slowly. That works beautifully — until rates bite, a few mega-customers wobble, or the GPUs you bought depreciate faster than the contract runs.

Here's the edge. When you sign a multi-year AI infrastructure deal, you are not just a customer. You are an unsecured creditor betting your vendor can finance the buildout your workload depends on. Underwrite them like one. Read the cash flow statement, not just the press release. Ask who's funding the capex behind your capacity, and what happens to your contract if that funding gets expensive.

A record backlog tells you AI is selling. It does not tell you AI is paid for. Those are two different bets — and right now the whole sector is making the second one.

I'm just calling balls and strikes.

AI is a race. Time to catch up.
