Your Vendor Has a Landlord

June 22, 2026 · 8:00 AM CT

Three days after Anthropic shipped its most capable model, it was gone. Not pulled by the lab. Pulled by the government.

Last week I wrote that access to the frontier had become conditional — gated by who you are and how you're vetted. This week it got worse. A federal export-control directive ordered Anthropic to cut off Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for every foreign national on earth, including its own employees. Rather than sort users by passport, the company switched both models off for everyone.

So the lesson is no longer "the best model may not be sold to you." It's "the model you already built on can be revoked overnight by a decision you don't control."

Read that for what it is. The risk to your AI roadmap stopped being price, and stopped being capability. It is now continuity. The system running your workflow answers to an export regime, a launch suspension, a policy shift you will not see coming. That is not a hypothetical. It happened in three days, to the most valuable AI company in the world.

Here is what executives need to internalize. A model you cannot keep is not a foundation. It is a rental, and the landlord just showed you he holds a key. If your stack assumes one frontier vendor, you have one point of political failure. The fix is not loyalty. It is portability — the ability to move work to another model the day the first one goes dark.

Build for the day the door closes. Because last week, it did.

It is a race. Time to catch up.
