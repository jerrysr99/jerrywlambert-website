Same Result, 12x The Cost

July 25, 2026

I ran the same image through two Claude models this morning. One cost twelve times the other. I could not tell the answers apart.

Evaluating an image on Opus 4.8 — the high-end model I reach for on hard problems — costs me about 3.6 cents. The same image on Haiku, the small fast model in the same family, costs about three tenths of a cent. Same picture. Same question. The answer I needed came back the same.

Run that at scale. A million images is roughly $36,000 on Opus and $2,900 on Haiku. Thirty-three thousand dollars, per million images, for a result I could not distinguish.

Here is what the sticker price hides. The rate-card gap between those two models is five times. I measured twelve. The premium model does not just cost more per token — it spends more tokens getting to the same answer. Both multipliers stack, and neither one shows up until the bill lands.

This is not a story about a cheap model beating an expensive one. Opus earns its price on the hard problems — the reasoning, the code, the judgment calls. It is a story about defaults. Most teams point every task at the biggest model they have and never look back. Evaluating an image is not a hard problem. Neither is classifying, extracting, tagging, or transcribing. That is most of the volume, and the cheap tier finishes it.

You do not need a second vendor to capture this. The gap is sitting inside the one you already use.

The best model for the task is rarely the best model you have. Unrestricted spending is being disguised as on unrestricted model selection.

AI is a race. Time to catch up.
