Every Vendor Counts Tokens Differently

June 29, 2026 · 9:30 AM CT

Send the exact same prompt to three AI vendors and you get back three different token counts. Three different invoices. For the same words.

That isn't a rounding error. It's the unit itself.

Every vendor tokenizes differently. OpenAI uses one scheme, Google another, Anthropic its own. The same paragraph can be 20% more tokens on one platform than another — and on two of the big three you can't even count it yourself before you send it. You learn the real number from the bill.

It gets worse downstream. Output costs three to six times input. Reasoning models bill you for "thinking" tokens you never see in the response. Caching is a different line item at every vendor. So when finance tries to tie a usage log to an invoice across vendors, the numbers don't meet — because the unit doesn't.

I've run large cost programs. You cannot manage what you cannot reconcile.

The discipline whose entire job is making bills tie out just said the same thing out loud. This month the FinOps Foundation shipped its first standard built for invoice reconciliation — and openly punted AI token tracking to a later version due in December. The same week, the Linux Foundation launched a new effort just to standardize the economics of AI. 98% of FinOps teams now manage AI spend. Two years ago it was 31%.

Here's the edge. We priced AI by the token before anyone agreed what a token is. If value is metered per token but the token isn't fungible across vendors, every multi-model bill is a reconciliation problem by design.

Don't standardize on one vendor to make the math easy. Standardize your own unit of value — cost per task, per resolution, per outcome — and make every vendor map to it.

AI is a race. Time to catch up.
