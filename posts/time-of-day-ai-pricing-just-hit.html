Time Of Day AI Pricing Just Hit

August 25, 2026

DeepSeek just made the same token cost two different prices depending on when you use it. Peak hours, full rate. Off-peak, half off. Same request, same model, different bill.

The reason is not greed. It is capacity. Nobody can build data centers fast enough for the demand, so the next best thing is to move the demand. Charge more when the servers are full. Charge less when they are empty. Push the flexible work into the quiet hours. Power companies have priced this way for a century. Airlines do it with every seat. One model maker already had to freeze new signups this summer when a launch overran its own hardware in 48 hours.

I expect more vendors to follow. When you cannot add supply, you manage demand with price. It is the only lever left.

Here is what it does to you. Checking an AI bill was already hard. One request is billed across different token classes, input, output, and cached, each at its own rate, in a mix that changes every call. That was the hard version. Now it gets harder. The move is to price by the time of day and the day of the week you ran the work. The rate depends on the exact minute each request landed, measured in a time zone that is not yours, against peak windows the vendor defines and can move. To check one invoice you now need a timestamp on every call, the vendor's peak calendar for that day, and the math to match each request to the rate that applied. Miss a daylight-saving shift and your numbers drift.

The price is no longer a number. It is a function of time, and the vendor owns the clock.

You cannot reconcile a bill against a rate that changes by the hour unless you keep your own timestamped record of every call. If you are not recording when each request happened, you cannot check the bill at all. You just pay it.

The meter now moves with the clock. Make sure the clock is also yours.
