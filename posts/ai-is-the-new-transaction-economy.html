AI Is the New Transaction Economy

June 26, 2026 · 8:00 AM CT

Somewhere in the last hour, one AI agent paid another a fraction of a cent. No invoice. No human in the loop. No card swipe.

For thirty years we bought software by the seat. That era is ending. AI is not just a tool you license — it is a transaction economy you operate inside, priced by the action, the token, and the result.

The plumbing is being laid in public. Two weeks ago Mastercard launched Agent Pay for Machines: a protocol for agents to pay each other autonomously, in amounts smaller than a single cent, with thirty-one partners from Stripe to Coinbase. Stripe has rebuilt its own stack as "the economic infrastructure for AI." And on its last earnings call, NVIDIA stopped talking about chips and started talking about tokens — inference is the revenue, and cost-per-token is the unit.

The pricing model is flipping with it. Salesforce is moving from charging for a seat to charging when its agent actually resolves the case. Intercom already bills ninety-nine cents per resolution. You pay for the outcome, not the login.

Here is what the announcements gloss over. The rails are being built faster than the demand has arrived — OpenAI quietly pulled back its in-chat checkout this spring. The transaction economy is not fully here yet. But the unit of value has already changed.

If you still budget AI by seats and annual licenses, you are using a subscription mental model to price a metered world. Re-architect the budget before the meter does it for you.

It is a race. Time to catch up.
