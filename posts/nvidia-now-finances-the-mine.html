Nvidia Now Finances the Mine

July 10, 2026 · 8:00 AM CT

Nvidia just agreed to buy back its own chips if its customers cannot sell the compute. Read that twice.

Here is the thing almost nobody is saying out loud. When the company selling the picks also finances the mine and guarantees the buyer against a dry hole, demand stops being an independent signal. It becomes something the seller is manufacturing.

On July 1, Nvidia launched a financing model for the cloud companies buying its GPUs. If those GPUs sit idle, Nvidia rents or buys back the unused capacity at a set floor price. In exchange, Nvidia takes a cut of the revenue those same chips earn when they are working. Two income streams on one sale. The hardware margin, and a royalty on the cloud built with it.

The first deals total about 210,000 GPUs. Sharon AI and Firmus. Sharon's backstop alone is valued at 4.88 billion dollars, on a six-year term.

The analysts at SemiAnalysis call the package capital, offtake, and datacenters bundled into one. They think it could seed a 7 trillion dollar AI debt market. In their words, Nvidia is starting to look like a central bank for AI.

Now stand where the enterprise buyer stands. A backlog financed by the seller is not the same as a backlog paid for by independent customers. When your vendor underwrites its own demand, the market signal you use to judge whether this is real gets quieter.

I wrote recently that signing a multi-year AI deal makes you an unsecured creditor. This is the other side of that coin. The manufacturer and the lender are now the same company, and it has every reason to keep the build going whether the end demand shows up or not.

I am not saying that is good or bad. Just calling balls and strikes. But if you are betting your roadmap on an AI vendor, underwrite the financing, not just the capability. Ask who is actually paying for the buildout your workload depends on. If the answer traces back to the chipmaker, that is concentration risk no spec sheet will show you.

AI is a race. Time to catch up.
